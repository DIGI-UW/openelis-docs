OpenELIS 3.3.0. This page describes the Locations & Organizations admin introduced in 3.3.0 (not yet released at the time of writing). Sites on 3.2.x and earlier use the older Organization Management screen: see Admin: Manage organizations (3.2.x).

At a glance

PurposeAdd and maintain the organizations your laboratory works with (the hospitals, clinics and health centres that send samples, and the laboratories you refer samples to) so they can be chosen in order entry and referrals.
You'll end up withAn active organization record with its type, codes, location and contact details, and any wards or departments inside it.
Who does thisAdministrators. Access is set by user permissions and can be configured by your administrator.
Before you startThe organization's name and what it does (requests tests, receives referrals, and so on). Have its codes ready if it has them, for example a DHIS2 ID. For a referral laboratory, know its approval status.
WhereMain menu → Admin → Locations & Organizations → Organizations

The Organizations page lists every organization OpenELIS knows about. Each row shows the organization's code, type, location, how many wards or departments it has, whether it is in use, and whether it is active. From here you add organizations, edit them, record their wards and departments, and switch them off when they are no longer used. Organizations appear in order entry as the requesting site and in referrals as the destination lab.

Places where environmental or vector samples are collected are sampling sites, not organizations, and are managed under Sampling Sites in the same menu.

Step 1: Open Organizations and find an organization

  1. Open Main menu → Admin → Locations & Organizations → Organizations. The list shows active organizations. Its columns are Name, Code, Type, Location, Wards / Depts, In use, Active and Actions. In use shows a total with the number still open in brackets. Under Type, the organization's category and ownership appear next to its type.

    Figure 1. The Organizations list, with the search box, filters and the Import, Export and Add buttons.

  2. Not sure whether you need an organization or a sampling site? Open Organization or sampling site: which do I need? under the page description. It explains the difference with examples.

    Figure 2. The "which do I need?" guidance, opened.

  3. To find an organization, type in Search by name, code or any identifier. Search matches the name, the code and every other identifier on the record. When the match is on an identifier other than the code, the identifier is shown under the name. Narrow the list further with Type, Location (includes areas beneath), Category, Ownership and Status (Active, Inactive or All).

    Figure 3. Searching by a DHIS2 ID finds Gerehu General Hospital and shows the matching identifier under its name.

Step 2: Add an organization

  1. Click Add. A form opens in the list, with links at the top to jump to its sections: Identity, Identifiers, Location and Contact.

  2. Under Identity, enter the Name (required) and, if you want, a Short name. Choose one or more Organization type(s) (required). The chosen types appear as tags under the box. Set Category and Ownership if you know them, and add a Description.

    Figure 4. The Identity section for a new urban clinic, typed as a referring clinic.

  3. Under Identifiers, the first row is labelled Code and is marked as the Reporting code. Enter the organization's code in Value. To record another identifier, click Add identifier, type a label (or pick one already used, such as DHIS2 ID) and enter the value. The reporting code is what shows as Code in lists, exports and reports; every identifier is searchable.

    Figure 5. A reporting code and a DHIS2 ID recorded for the clinic.

  4. Under Location, pick the most specific Location (area) you know, then fill in Street address, City, State / Province, Postal code, and GPS latitude and GPS longitude in decimal degrees. Under Contact, add a Contact name, Phone, Email and Website as available. All of these are optional.

    Figure 6. Address, GPS position and contact details for the clinic.

  5. If you chose the referralLab type, a Referral laboratory · ISO 15189 6.8 section appears below Contact. Approval status is required (Approved, Under evaluation, Suspended or Not approved). You can also record the Accreditation body, Accreditation number, Accreditation expiry, Last review date, Next review due and Review notes. Without an approval status the record is not saved and the page shows Not saved yet. Check: Approval status.

    Figure 7. The Referral laboratory section for a referral lab, with its approval status and review dates.

  6. Click Save. A message confirms the organization was added, and the new record stays open for editing.

    Figure 8. Confirmation that Tokarara Urban Clinic was added.

Step 3: Edit an organization and add wards or departments

  1. Find the organization and click Edit in its row. The same form opens, with an extra Wards / Depts section. Change any field and click Save. Close in the row closes the form.

  2. To add a ward, department or clinic inside the organization, click Add ward / dept in the Wards / Depts section. Enter its name, an optional code, and a Service type (Inpatient, Outpatient, Intensive care, Emergency, Maternity, Laboratory or Other). Contact name, phone and email are optional. A ward uses the organization's address, and its GPS position too unless you give it one.

    Figure 9. A new Outpatient Department being added to Gerehu General Hospital.

  3. Click Save 1 new ward / dept (the button counts the new rows). Wards are saved with this button, not with the main Save. If you press the main Save while a new ward is still unsaved, the page says 1 ward / dept is typed but not saved yet and saves nothing until you save or remove it.

    Figure 10. The saved ward, with its GPS position inherited from the hospital.

  4. To see what has changed on a record, click History at the bottom of the form. The history lists each change with its date, the user who made it and the old and new values. Use All changes to filter by kind of change. The history is read-only.

    Figure 11. The history of Gerehu General Hospital.

Step 4: Deactivate or reactivate an organization

  1. Click the Active switch in the organization's row. The change takes effect immediately, with no confirmation step. A message confirms it and offers Undo. With the Status filter on Active, the organization leaves the list.

    Figure 12. Tokarara Urban Clinic deactivated, with Undo in the message.

  2. To find inactive organizations, set Status to Inactive or All. Click the switch again to reactivate one.

    Figure 13. The Status filter set to Inactive, showing the deactivated clinic.

Organization types

Types decide where an organization can be chosen. An organization can have more than one.

TypeUse it for
referring clinicA site that requests tests from your laboratory. It appears as the requesting site in order entry.
referralLabA laboratory you send samples to for tests you do not perform. Adds the Referral laboratory section.
patient referralThe clinic that referred the patient to your laboratory.
Health District, Health RegionAdministrative health areas.
TestKitVenderA supplier of test kits.

Tips and notes

  • Organizations are deactivated, not deleted. Inactive records are hidden from order entry and other pickers, but still appear on existing orders and reports.
  • If you deactivate the wrong organization, click Undo in the message straight away.
  • The Organization type(s) box shows a count of the types chosen; the type names appear as tags under it. This is a known issue (OGC-1420).
  • While a new ward row is open, the page may scroll sideways on smaller screens. This is a known layout issue.
