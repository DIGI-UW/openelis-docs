This page describes OpenELIS 3.3.0. Sites on 3.2.x see an earlier version of the signing dialogs; see Electronic Signatures (before 3.3.0).

How-to · Administrators (to enable) and result-entry and validation staff (to sign)

At a glance

PurposeTurn on electronic signatures and use them to sign result entry and validation, supporting 21 CFR Part 11 accountability.
You'll end up withResult entry and validation that require the user's electronic signature, recorded with the action and its meaning.
Who does thisAn administrator enables it once; result-entry and validation staff then sign. Access is set by user permissions and can be configured by your administrator.
Before you startDecide that your laboratory requires electronic signatures. Each signing user needs their own OpenELIS login (Admin → User Management), with the Results or Validation role for their lab units.
WhereEnable: Main menu → Admin → General Configurations → Site Information. Sign: Main menu → Results → Results Entry, and Main menu → Validation. Review: Main menu → Quality Assurance → QMS & Improvement → Electronic Signature Log.

Electronic signatures are off by default. An administrator turns them on with one Site Information setting. After that, every save in result entry and validation asks the user to sign. The first time a user signs, they certify once that their electronic signature is legally binding. The first signature in each session needs their username and password; later signatures in the same session need only the password.

Step 1: Enable electronic signatures (administrator)

  1. Open Main menu → Admin → General Configurations → Site Information. Find electronicSignatureEnabled (Enable 21 CFR Part 11 compliant electronic signatures for result entry and validation workflows) and, below it, esigSessionTimeoutMinutes (E-signature session inactivity timeout in minutes (default: 30)).

    {{IMG:01-site-information.png}}

    Figure 1. The two e-signature settings in Site Information, here set to true and 30 minutes.

  2. Select the electronicSignatureEnabled row and click Modify. The Edit Record page shows the name, description and Value.

  3. Choose True and click Save. Exit leaves without saving.

    {{IMG:02-edit-record.png}}

    Figure 2. Edit Record for electronicSignatureEnabled.

  4. Optionally change esigSessionTimeoutMinutes the same way. It sets how many minutes of inactivity end a signing session, after which the next signature asks for the username again.

Step 2: Certify, the first time you sign

  1. Enter or validate results as usual and click Save.

  2. If you have never signed before, the Electronic Signature Certification dialog opens. It explains that you must certify that your electronic signature is the legally binding equivalent of your handwritten signature, followed by the statement you are agreeing to.

  3. Tick I have read and understand the above statement, enter your Password, and click Certify & Continue. The button stays unavailable until both are done. Cancel closes the dialog without saving.

    {{IMG:03-certification.png}}

    Figure 3. A technician's first signature: the certification statement acknowledged and the password entered.

Certification is a one-time step for each user. After it, the signature dialog in Step 3 opens directly.

Step 3: Sign

  1. The Electronic Signature dialog shows who is signing (Signing as), the Action being signed (for example Save DEV01260000000000505 - GPT/ALAT(Serum)) and the Signature Meaning (Authored for result entry).

  2. For your first signature in a session, the dialog says This is your first signature in this session. Please enter your username and password to authenticate. Enter your Username and Password and click Sign.

    {{IMG:04-first-signature.png}}

    Figure 4. The first signature in a session asks for username and password.

  3. For later signatures in the same session, the dialog says You have an active signing session. Enter your password to continue. Your username is shown and only the Password is needed.

    {{IMG:05-session-signature.png}}

    Figure 5. Later signatures in the session ask only for the password.

  4. The save completes. In result entry the row's status becomes Accepted by technician.

    {{IMG:06-saved.png}}

    Figure 6. The signed result saved.

Every dialog ends with the reminder: By entering your credentials, you are creating a legally binding electronic signature per 21 CFR Part 11.

Step 4: Review the signature log

Every signature is recorded in the Electronic Signature Log, for audits and inspections.

  1. Open Main menu → Quality Assurance → QMS & Improvement → Electronic Signature Log. The log lists every signature with Signed At, Signer, Action (the signature meaning, for example Authored or Validated & Released), Subject (what was signed, for example a result or a validation batch) and Reason. It opens on the last 30 days.

    {{IMG:07-signature-log.png}}

    Figure 7. The Electronic Signature Log, newest first.

  2. Narrow the list with Date Range (From) and (To), Action (Authored, Validated & Released, Rejected), Subject Type and User, then select Apply Filters. Clear Filters resets them.

    {{IMG:09-log-filtered.png}}

    Figure 8. The log filtered to one technician's signatures.

  3. Select Export CSV or Export PDF to download what the filters show. With no filters applied, the page first asks Export without filters?, because the export then holds every signature in the date range (up to 10,000 rows); select Export to go ahead.

    {{IMG:08-export-confirm.png}}

    Figure 9. Confirming an export with no filters.

    The PDF is titled Electronic Signature Log and lists the date range, the number of records and when it was generated, then the same columns as the screen.

    {{IMG:10-log-pdf.png}}

    Figure 10. The exported PDF.

Note: the exported CSV and PDF give times in UTC, while the screen shows local time; the Signer filter lists users as Last name, First name; and Subject Type shows internal codes (RESULT, VALIDATION_BATCH and so on). The PDF's Laboratory line was blank on the test server.

Field reference

FieldNotes
electronicSignatureEnabledSite Information setting. True turns on electronic signatures for result entry and validation. Default false.
esigSessionTimeoutMinutesMinutes of inactivity before a signing session ends. Default 30.
I have read and understand the above statementRequired acknowledgement on the one-time certification dialog.
Username, PasswordThe signer's own OpenELIS credentials. Username is asked for on the first signature of a session only.
Signature MeaningWhat the signature means for the action, shown in the dialog. Result entry signs as Authored.

Notes

  • Make sure everyone who will sign has their own login before go-live, and ask them to certify on their first save.
  • Turning electronicSignatureEnabled back to False removes the prompts.
  • Validators who release several Clear-lane results at once sign once for all of them when allowBulkReleaseClear is true (Result Entry Configuration). See the validation pages.
